Thatch -- what is counted, and how
==================================

Tolerance on the rates belongs to the measuring method, not the turn. When
the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older rates
figures were left as they stood.

The rates are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the thatch.

The queue at bundling runs oldest first and exceptions are logged. The
exception log at the thatch is reviewed before the exceptions are: a rule
broken often is a rule written wrong.

The bundling station is swept between cycles. What the sweeping takes away
is not waste but the previous cycle's leavings, and the thatch ledger has
confused the two before.

Night turns at the thatch are logged separately. Folding them into the day
figure once hid a standing shortfall in the rates for most of a quarter
before anyone looked.

Conditions at bundling are logged at the start and the end of a turn,
never averaged across the middle. An average hides a thatch drifting
steadily in one direction.

The thatch keeps the superseded figure alongside the corrected one for a
full cycle. A correction that erases its predecessor leaves the rates
series looking as though it never moved.

Two people witness the frame being zeroed before the first rates are
measured. A zero witnessed by one person has failed twice at the thatch,
both times in the same direction.

Kept as a plain record of the rates. Written as a program it would give
the roster's parsers something to read at the thatch.
