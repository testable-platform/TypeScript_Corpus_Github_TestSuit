Marl -- what is counted, and how
================================

Conditions at digging are logged at the start and the end of a turn, never
averaged across the middle. An average hides a marl drifting steadily in
one direction.

The digging station is swept between cycles. What the sweeping takes away
is not waste but the previous cycle's leavings, and the marl ledger has
confused the two before.

The queue at digging runs oldest first and exceptions are logged. The
exception log at the marl is reviewed before the exceptions are: a rule
broken often is a rule written wrong.

Tolerance on the pits belongs to the measuring method, not the turn. When
the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older pits
figures were left as they stood.

A lot rejected at digging is held, not returned the same day. The marl
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the pits were
rejected at digging in the first place.

A holding of pits reviewed and left in place is marked reviewed. The marl
no longer distinguishes reviewed from unreviewed by silence, having done
so for most of one season.

The marl keeps the superseded figure alongside the corrected one for a
full cycle. A correction that erases its predecessor leaves the pits
series looking as though it never moved.

Who counted the pits is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion blame
at the marl, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a measurement
worth keeping.

Nothing in the marl file executes and nothing declares a project. For the
pits that is the only property here that matters.
