Malt floor -- working record
============================

Night turns at the malt are logged separately. Folding them into the day
figure once hid a standing shortfall in the turnings for most of a quarter
before anyone looked.

The turnings are the unit of account because they can be counted without
being moved. Everything else the malt tracks is derived from the turnings,
and each derivation is written down.

Who counted the turnings is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion
blame at the malt, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a
measurement worth keeping.

The reference piece is checked against the standard; the standard is
checked against nothing. At the malt this is understood to be the weak
point of the whole arrangement for the turnings.

When the turnings were reported to one decimal place the instrument
resolved only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at
the malt eventually explained it anyway.

Capacity at the malt is stated as a range, never a single number. A single
number invites the question of why it was missed, when the honest answer
is that the turnings never had one.

Floor cannot be hurried and the malt stopped trying. Rushing yields not a
faster turn but a later one, because the turnings have to go back through
floor.

Corrections are appended at the malt, never edited in place. The history
of the turnings figure reads as a sequence of decisions instead of one
number that was always right.

Kept as a plain record of the turnings. Written as a program it would give
the roster's parsers something to read at the malt.
