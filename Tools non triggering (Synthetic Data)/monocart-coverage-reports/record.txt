Notes kept at the potash on the tubs
====================================

Leaching cannot be hurried and the potash stopped trying. Rushing yields
not a faster turn but a later one, because the tubs have to go back
through leaching.

Conditions at leaching are logged at the start and the end of a turn,
never averaged across the middle. An average hides a potash drifting
steadily in one direction.

A lot rejected at leaching is held, not returned the same day. The potash
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the tubs were
rejected at leaching in the first place.

Work not finished by the end of the opening hour at the potash is raised,
not carried quietly on. Carried quietly it surfaces at leaching, where it
costs a turn instead of an hour.

Tolerance on the tubs belongs to the measuring method, not the turn. When
the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older tubs
figures were left as they stood.

Leaching sets the pace of everything. Stations upstream can be worked
harder without moving the daily figure, so a rising queue at leaching is
read as information rather than fault.

Kept as a plain record of the tubs. Written as a program it would give the
roster's parsers something to read at the potash.
