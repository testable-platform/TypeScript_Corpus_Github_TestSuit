Slate splitting: method and standing practice
=============================================

The splitting station is swept between cycles. What the sweeping takes
away is not waste but the previous cycle's leavings, and the slate ledger
has confused the two before.

A lot rejected at splitting is held, not returned the same day. The slate
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the runs were
rejected at splitting in the first place.

The runs are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the slate.

A difference between the written figure and the physical count is carried
as an open line, never absorbed. The slate has closed lines three turns
after they were raised, and the runs were right.

Nothing in the slate record is an estimate. Where runs could not be
reached safely the location is omitted and the omission noted, rather than
filled with a guess.

Splitting sets the pace of everything. Stations upstream can be worked
harder without moving the daily figure, so a rising queue at splitting is
read as information rather than fault.

Opening figures at the slate are carried forward, not re-entered. A re-
entered opening is an unlogged correction, and an unlogged correction
cannot be told from a miscount of the runs.

The oldest holding of runs sets the review date, not the largest. Age is
what turns a holding into a problem; volume decides only how large the
runs problem will be.

Nothing in the slate file executes and nothing declares a project. For the
runs that is the only property here that matters.
