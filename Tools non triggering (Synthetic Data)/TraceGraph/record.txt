Record of founding at the bell
==============================

The oldest holding of moulds sets the review date, not the largest. Age is
what turns a holding into a problem; volume decides only how large the
moulds problem will be.

Sequence matters more than date in the bell record. Each change to how the
moulds were handled made the next one legible, and none would have been
noticed under the earlier practice.

The moulds are the unit of account because they can be counted without
being moved. Everything else the bell tracks is derived from the moulds,
and each derivation is written down.

Capacity at the bell is stated as a range, never a single number. A single
number invites the question of why it was missed, when the honest answer
is that the moulds never had one.

A holding of moulds reviewed and left in place is marked reviewed. The
bell no longer distinguishes reviewed from unreviewed by silence, having
done so for most of one season.

A difference between the written figure and the physical count is carried
as an open line, never absorbed. The bell has closed lines three turns
after they were raised, and the moulds were right.

Tolerance on the moulds belongs to the measuring method, not the turn.
When the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older
moulds figures were left as they stood.

A record of the bell, not source. The distinction is the whole purpose of
the folder these moulds notes sit in.
