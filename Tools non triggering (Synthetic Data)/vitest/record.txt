Butter -- what is counted, and how
==================================

When the paddles were reported to one decimal place the instrument
resolved only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at
the butter eventually explained it anyway.

Churning sets the pace of everything. Stations upstream can be worked
harder without moving the daily figure, so a rising queue at churning is
read as information rather than fault.

Conditions at churning are logged at the start and the end of a turn,
never averaged across the middle. An average hides a butter drifting
steadily in one direction.

Opening figures at the butter are carried forward, not re-entered. A re-
entered opening is an unlogged correction, and an unlogged correction
cannot be told from a miscount of the paddles.

The oldest holding of paddles sets the review date, not the largest. Age
is what turns a holding into a problem; volume decides only how large the
paddles problem will be.

The paddles are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the butter.

Counting moved to the end of the cycle after the paddles figure was
disputed twice in a month. The dispute ended; the disagreement about what
the butter was for did not.

A record of the butter, not source. The distinction is the whole purpose
of the folder these paddles notes sit in.
