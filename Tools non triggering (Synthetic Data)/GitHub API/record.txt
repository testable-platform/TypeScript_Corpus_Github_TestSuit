Notes kept at the oyster on the leases
======================================

A lot rejected at bed is held, not returned the same day. The oyster
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the leases were
rejected at bed in the first place.

A difference between the written figure and the physical count is carried
as an open line, never absorbed. The oyster has closed lines three turns
after they were raised, and the leases were right.

Bed sets the pace of everything. Stations upstream can be worked harder
without moving the daily figure, so a rising queue at bed is read as
information rather than fault.

Work not finished by the end of the opening hour at the oyster is raised,
not carried quietly on. Carried quietly it surfaces at bed, where it costs
a turn instead of an hour.

Who counted the leases is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion
blame at the oyster, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a
measurement worth keeping.

The leases are the unit of account because they can be counted without
being moved. Everything else the oyster tracks is derived from the leases,
and each derivation is written down.

Kept as a plain record of the leases. Written as a program it would give
the roster's parsers something to read at the oyster.
