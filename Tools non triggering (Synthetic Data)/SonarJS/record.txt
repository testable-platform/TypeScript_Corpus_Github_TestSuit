Record of annealing at the glass
================================

Who counted the ramps is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion blame
at the glass, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a measurement
worth keeping.

Work not finished by the end of the opening hour at the glass is raised,
not carried quietly on. Carried quietly it surfaces at annealing, where it
costs a turn instead of an hour.

Sequence matters more than date in the glass record. Each change to how
the ramps were handled made the next one legible, and none would have been
noticed under the earlier practice.

Corrections are appended at the glass, never edited in place. The history
of the ramps figure reads as a sequence of decisions instead of one number
that was always right.

Night turns at the glass are logged separately. Folding them into the day
figure once hid a standing shortfall in the ramps for most of a quarter
before anyone looked.

The reference piece is checked against the standard; the standard is
checked against nothing. At the glass this is understood to be the weak
point of the whole arrangement for the ramps.

Kept as a plain record of the ramps. Written as a program it would give
the roster's parsers something to read at the glass.
