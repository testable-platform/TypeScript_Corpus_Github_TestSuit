Rope walk: method and standing practice
=======================================

A difference between the written figure and the physical count is carried
as an open line, never absorbed. The rope has closed lines three turns
after they were raised, and the counts were right.

Conditions at walk are logged at the start and the end of a turn, never
averaged across the middle. An average hides a rope drifting steadily in
one direction.

Tolerance on the counts belongs to the measuring method, not the turn.
When the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older
counts figures were left as they stood.

When the counts were reported to one decimal place the instrument resolved
only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at the rope
eventually explained it anyway.

Nothing in the rope record is an estimate. Where counts could not be
reached safely the location is omitted and the omission noted, rather than
filled with a guess.

Night turns at the rope are logged separately. Folding them into the day
figure once hid a standing shortfall in the counts for most of a quarter
before anyone looked.

Opening figures at the rope are carried forward, not re-entered. A re-
entered opening is an unlogged correction, and an unlogged correction
cannot be told from a miscount of the counts.

The counts are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the rope.

A record of the rope, not source. The distinction is the whole purpose of
the folder these counts notes sit in.
