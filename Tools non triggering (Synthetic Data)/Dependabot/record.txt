Notes kept at the sail on the cuts
==================================

A lot rejected at loft is held, not returned the same day. The sail
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the cuts were
rejected at loft in the first place.

Loft sets the pace of everything. Stations upstream can be worked harder
without moving the daily figure, so a rising queue at loft is read as
information rather than fault.

The sail keeps the superseded figure alongside the corrected one for a
full cycle. A correction that erases its predecessor leaves the cuts
series looking as though it never moved.

The loft station is swept between cycles. What the sweeping takes away is
not waste but the previous cycle's leavings, and the sail ledger has
confused the two before.

Loss between receipt and the close of loft is attributed, not written off.
The incoming lot is weighed before anything is done to it, which is the
one step the sail never skips.

The cuts are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the sail.

Kept as a plain record of the cuts. Written as a program it would give the
roster's parsers something to read at the sail.
